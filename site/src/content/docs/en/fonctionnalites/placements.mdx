Nid tracks each of the household's savings and investment envelopes: its value, what went in, what it gained and what its fees cost. Returns account for the dates of each contribution, and gains are shown before tax.

## The questions it answers

- What are our investments worth today, and how much did we put in?
- What really earns, once the fees are paid?
- What do the fees cost over 10 or 20 years?
- How much room is left under the Livret A ceiling?

## What Nid does

- **Envelopes**: PEA, life insurance, PER, regulated savings accounts, brokerage accounts, current accounts. Their list gathers those of the same person, or the same couple, on one row with their total, to unfold.
- **Values and movements**: contributions, withdrawals, valuations, and a curve of the value next to the contributions.
- **The holdings of each envelope** and the switches between funds.
- **The annual return** of each envelope, which accounts for the date of every contribution.
- **The fees** and their cumulative cost over 10 and 20 years.
- **The ceilings** of regulated savings accounts, and what can still go in.
- **Statements** can be proposed by an assistant or from the bank, then approved line by line.

## The terms

- **Envelope**: the container of a saving: a PEA, a life insurance contract, a savings account. Each has its own rules and tax treatment.
- **PEA**: French equity savings plan, to invest in European shares with lighter tax after 5 years.
- **PER**: French retirement savings plan: contributions can lower income tax, the money is in principle locked until retirement.
- **Livret A, LDDS**: French regulated savings accounts, tax-free, with a contribution ceiling.
- **Annual return**: the rate that would have produced the same gain, accounting for when each sum went in (the XIRR method).
- **Switch**: money moved from one fund to another inside the same envelope.
