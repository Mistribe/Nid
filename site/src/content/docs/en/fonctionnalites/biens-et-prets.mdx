Nid tracks the household's property and the loans that finance it. Each loan's schedule is computed by the server and checked against 3,000 reference loans; you can compare it with your bank's table.

## The questions it answers

- How much is left to repay, and until when?
- What would an early repayment change: a lower payment or a shorter term?
- What is our property worth, once the loan is deducted?
- How much does the rental earn, charges and vacancy included?

## What Nid does

- **The property's page**: net value, unrealised capital gain, real cost in detail, rental yield.
- **The list of properties** gathers those of the same person, or the same couple, on one row with their value and net value, to unfold; a switch puts each back on its own row.
- **The loan schedule**: interest and principal in each payment, principal left, phases (deferral, zero-rate loan), total cost.
- **The bank's table** side by side with Nid's computation.
- **Early repayment and renegotiation**, simulated before you decide.
- **The household's debt ratio**, and the end date of each loan.
- **Rentals**: leases, rent revision by the IRL, receipts, unpaid rents (a month is unpaid once it is over) and a reminder letter.

## The terms

- **Principal left**: the part of the loan still to repay, without the interest to come.
- **Schedule**: the table of each monthly payment, split into interest, principal and insurance; also called the amortization table.
- **Debt ratio**: the share of income spent on loans, rents counted at 70 % as banks do; French banks often look at a 35 % limit.
- **PTZ**: French zero-rate loan, state-backed, for buying a main home.
- **IRL**: French rent reference index, published by INSEE every quarter, which caps the yearly revision of a rent.
