# Subscription Tracking — Data Model

This document describes the data model used to register subscriptions and track their cost over time. It is the reference for implementation: entities, rules, derived computations and how real-world offers map onto the model.

## Goals

- Register any kind of subscription offer: simple recurring billing, prepaid multi-year terms, bonus months, bundles that include subscription time, gift cards, trials, intro promotions, price changes.
- Know at any date whether a subscription is covered, until when, and what the next charge will be.
- Compute cost two ways: **cash cost** (what left the account, and when) and **amortized cost** (what each month of service actually cost).

## Core principle

A subscription is described by three separate concepts that must never be mixed in the same record:

1. **Subscription**: the long-lived relationship with a service (identity, owner, users). It survives renewals, price changes and plan switches.
2. **Money**: what was paid, by whom, and when. Stored as `BillingPhase` (recurring rules) and `Purchase` (one-off payments).
3. **Coverage**: the periods during which the service was accessible, and why (paid, bonus, included, trial, gift). Always **derived**, never entered by hand.

One payment can grant several coverage periods (paid + bonus), and one coverage period can come from a purchase that is not a subscription at all (a game expansion including 1 month). This separation is what makes all the offer variations fit the same model.

## Conventions

- **Dates** are calendar dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Intervals are **half-open** `[start, end)`: `end` is the first day *not* covered. Never use inclusive end dates.
- **Durations** are `{ count, unit }` with `unit` in `day | week | month | year`. No enum like `monthly`/`quarterly`/`custom`: "every 4 months" is `{ count: 4, unit: "month" }`. Durations are never converted to a number of days, because months vary in length.
- **Month arithmetic** keeps an anchor day and clamps to the end of the month: starting on Jan 31, +1 month = Feb 28 (or 29), +2 months = Mar 31.
- **Money** is `{ amount, currency }` with `amount` as a decimal type (never floating point) and `currency` as an ISO 4217 code. Amounts are stored in their original currency; conversion only happens at reporting time, using the rate at the payment date.
- **Negative amounts** are allowed only on `adjustment` and `refund` purchases.

## Entities

### Subscription

Stable identity of a service relationship.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | id | Unique. One subscription per service relationship, not per period. |
| `provider_key` | string? | Key of a `Provider` (catalog or custom), e.g. `spotify`. Null = no provider. See "Provider catalog". |
| `name` | string | Display name, e.g. "Spotify Premium Duo" |
| `service_users` | `[person_id]` | Who actually uses it: a `person` of the Core, possibly a `dependent` |
| `auto_renew` | bool | Whether open billing phases keep generating charges |
| `cancelled_at` | date? | After this date, no new charges are generated. Existing coverage runs until its end. |
| `note` | string? | Free text, no business effect |

A subscription is a Core `item` (`module = subscriptions`, `kind = subscription`). Its owners, their shares and its visibility are held by the Core (`ownership`, `item.visibility`, see `core-data-model.md` §4), not by fields of this entity. A former `family` owner becomes both adults at 50/50.

A price change, a plan upgrade or a renewal does **not** create a new subscription. Switching to a genuinely different service (e.g. moving from Spotify to Deezer) does.

### BillingPhase

A recurring pricing rule: "while this phase is active, each renewal costs X and covers N units".

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | id | |
| `subscription_id` | id | |
| `starts_on` | date | First possible charge date |
| `ends_on` | date? | `null` = still running. A price change closes the current phase and opens a new one. |
| `price` | money | Price of one renewal |
| `every` | duration | Billing period, e.g. `{1, month}` |
| `covers` | duration? | Coverage granted per renewal. Defaults to `every`. |
| `max_cycles` | int? | Limits the number of renewals (intro offers: "3 months at 0.99"). |
| `payer` | `{ type: person \| household, person_id? }` | Who pays. `household` = paid by the household (formerly `family`). `person_id` is set only for `person`. |
| `note` | string? | Free text, no business effect |

Phases of the same subscription must not overlap in time.

**Important:** a phase does not charge on a fixed calendar. It charges **when coverage runs out** (see "Timeline algorithm"). This is what lets gift cards, bonus months and prepaid terms push back the next charge.

### Purchase

A single payment that is not generated by a phase.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | id | |
| `date` | date | Payment date |
| `label` | string | e.g. "NordVPN 2-year plan", "The War Within Epic Edition" |
| `price` | money | Total paid. `0` for a received gift. |
| `payer` | `{ type: person \| household \| external, person_id? }` | `person_id` is set only for `person`. `external` = someone outside the household (received gift). |
| `kind` | enum | `prepaid_term \| bundle \| gift \| adjustment \| refund` |
| `grants` | `[Grant]` | Coverage granted by this purchase (may be empty for adjustments/refunds) |
| `allocated_amount` | decimal? | For `bundle` only: part of the price counted as subscription cost. Defaults to `0` for bundles, to `price` for other kinds. Must be `<= price`. |
| `note` | string? | Free text, no business effect |

#### Grant

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `subscription_id` | id | |
| `duration` | duration | Length of the granted coverage |
| `source` | enum | `paid \| bonus \| included \| trial \| gift` |
| `starts_on` | date? | Explicit start. `null` = **stack** after the current coverage end (see below). |

### CoveragePeriod (derived)

Never stored as input. Rebuilt from phases and purchases; may be cached.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `subscription_id` | id | |
| `start`, `end` | date | Half-open interval |
| `source` | enum | `paid \| bonus \| included \| trial \| gift` |
| `origin` | `{ phase_id, cycle }` or `{ purchase_id, grant_index }` | Traceability back to the money |

### Charge (derived)

One occurrence of money leaving the account. Purchases are charges as-is. Phases produce one charge per renewal.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `date` | date | |
| `price` | money | |
| `payer` | `{ type, person_id? }` | Same shape as on phases and purchases |
| `origin` | `{ phase_id, cycle }` or `{ purchase_id }` | |
| `coverage` | `[CoveragePeriod]` | Periods this charge paid for (used for amortization) |

## Provider catalog

A provider gives a subscription its identity in the UI (name, icon, labels) and prefill suggestions. It carries **no billing logic**: money and coverage stay in phases and purchases.

### Provider

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `key` | string | Unique, lowercase, hyphenated (`spotify`, `nordvpn`). Same key space as the catalog. Catalog keys are used as published; a `custom` key is **generated**, never chosen by the caller: `<slug of the name>-<6 random [a-z0-9]>` (`tinder-k3f9qa`). |
| `name` | string | Display name. Optional localized variants: `default` plus `local` keyed by locale (`fr-fr`). |
| `origin` | enum | `catalog` (imported), `custom` (created by a user) or `adopted` (a key stored in `subscription.provider_key` before any catalog existed) |
| `website`, `pricing_page` | url? | `http`/`https` only |
| `icon_url` | url? | Declared by the catalog. `https` only, at most 2048 characters. |
| `icon_asset` | string? | SHA-256 of the stored WebP. `null` until fetched, or if the download failed or was never attempted. |
| `icon_source` | url? | The URL the stored asset was fetched from. `null` alongside `icon_asset`; differs from `icon_url` between the merge and the next successful icon pass. |
| `label_keys` | `[string]` | At least one |
| `plans` | json? | Not built for now (to be rethought before it is); not stored, not read |
| `catalog_commit` | string? | Commit the row was imported from |
| `archived_at` | date? | Set when a `catalog` provider leaves the catalog. Never deleted; its subscriptions keep it. |

Rules:

- `custom` providers are never overwritten by a sync. `catalog` providers are updated by a sync.
- A provider referenced by a subscription is never deleted: a `catalog` provider that leaves the catalog is archived and keeps its subscriptions.
- A custom key may not equal a catalog key: a generated key never collides with a catalog key by construction (catalog keys carry no random suffix), and the draw is retried on the rare accidental collision.
- **`adopted` providers.** Keys already stored in `subscription.provider_key` before the catalog existed (imported before 1C2b, or written through the API since 1C1) are adopted at the migration: one `adopted` provider per distinct stored key, name derived from the key (`wow` → "Wow"), label `other`, no website. At the first sync afterwards, an `adopted` provider whose key matches a catalog key **becomes** that `catalog` provider (same key, no subscription changes); those that stay `adopted` are listed in every sync report (§ "Catalog source" below) until someone moves their subscriptions to another provider.
- **Visibility is derived, never stored**: a `catalog` provider is visible to every adult; a `custom` or `adopted` provider is visible to an adult who can see a subscription using it; a `custom` provider is also visible to its creator while no subscription visible to them uses it. A provider the actor cannot see answers exactly like one that does not exist, on every path (list, write, import).

### Label

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `key` | string | Unique |
| `name` | string | Display name, localized in the UI by key (the catalog is single-language) |
| `color` | string | CSS color, `#RRGGBB` |
| `origin` | enum | `catalog` or `local` |

The catalog stores colors as `#AARRGGBB` (alpha first, Android style); they are converted to `#RRGGBB` at sync, dropping the alpha. A key appearing twice in the catalog file keeps its first occurrence; a category with no matching label gets one created for it (name from the key, neutral color). One `local` label exists, `other`, created for `adopted` providers.

### Catalog source

The reference catalog is the public repository `Mistribe/SubTracker-Data` (MIT): `providers.json`, `labels.json`, validated against `schemas/providersSchema.json` and `schemas/labelsSchema.json` (JSON Schema draft-07). At the time of review it holds **17 providers** and **19 labels** (one duplicate key, several identical colors) and a few illustration images. It is a **seed**, not an exhaustive directory: users are expected to create custom providers. The existing subscription data uses 8 provider keys (once the `s_` prefix is removed), and all 8 are in the catalog; World of Warcraft is filed under `battlenet`. Blizzard has no entry of its own, and Apple has only `apple-music`.

Catalog fields used:

| Catalog field | Provider field |
|---|---|
| `key`, `name` | `key`, `name` (with `local` variants) |
| `website`, `pricing_page` | same |
| `icon` | Read into `icon_url`: an `https` URL of at most 2048 characters, else ignored and reported (the provider is kept) |
| `categories` (18 allowed values) | `label_keys` |
| `plans[]` (`name`, `description`, `features`, `prices`, `recurrency`, `options`) | Ignored for now (1C2b §2): not stored, not read |

Sync rules:

- **Pinned**: the configuration names a commit (`Catalog:Commit`, default `882b195d7923ada66993548adda10791e05d198a`, the merge of `Mistribe/SubTracker-Data#5`, 2026-09-25). The sync is an explicit action — a durable job started at startup while no sync has ever succeeded, and on demand from Administration › Catalogue — never on a schedule (the commit is pinned, so a periodic run would only repeat the same version).
- **Validated on ingestion**: an entry that breaks a rule (key pattern, name empty or too long, URL not `http`/`https`, no category, malformed color) is refused and listed with its reason; the rest of the file is still written. A file that is not the expected JSON array fails the whole run and writes nothing.
- **Journal**: every sync is recorded in `catalog_sync_run` (`id`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `status`, `commit`, `failure_code`, `failure_message`, `report`), the same shape and rules as `core.fx_sync_run` (`core-data-model.md`): at most one running row, a run left running for an hour closed as abandoned, an interrupted run closed as interrupted, runs older than 400 days purged except the latest success. The report lists providers added, updated, unchanged and archived; adopted providers replaced by the catalog and adopted providers remaining; labels added, updated and created for a category without one; duplicate keys; refused entries with their reason. **The stored report is filtered for its reader**: an adopted-replaced, adopted-remaining or custom-conflict entry is shown only if the reader can see, at present, one of the subscriptions that used that key at sync time; a replaced key the reader cannot see is shown to them as added instead (what a household that never used it would have seen), so every catalog key of the run stays accounted for; a conflict the reader cannot see is dropped. No count of dropped entries is exposed. Only the system actor gets the complete, unfiltered report.
- Syncing the same commit twice changes nothing: every catalog key comes out unchanged.

### Icons

Catalog `icon` values are third-party URLs (a logo aggregator, Wikimedia). Displaying them straight from the browser would tell those hosts which providers the household uses, and the links can rot. Therefore, as built:

- **Downloaded after the merge**, outside its lock, one provider at a time in key order, for every non-archived catalog provider whose icon is missing or came from another URL: through the application's single outbound HTTP client (`security/application.md` §3), 15 s per icon.
- **Processed** from the content, never from the URL or the declared `Content-Type` (`IconProcessor`): PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF (first frame) accepted; SVG and anything else refused `unsupported_format`. At most 4096 × 4096 read from the header before any pixel is decoded; the EXIF orientation applied. Fitted within a 256 px square, ratio kept, never enlarged. Re-encoded as **lossless WebP** without metadata (EXIF, ICC, XMP all stripped); the SHA-256 of that WebP is its hash.
- **Stored** at `{root}/{first 2 hex chars}/{sha256}.webp` under `Assets:Root` (`.data/assets` in development, `/var/lib/nid/assets` in production).
- **Served** by `GET /api/v1/subscriptions/providers/icons/{hash}`, behind the session, `Cache-Control: private, max-age=31536000, immutable`, `ETag` = the hash, `If-None-Match` answering `304`.
- **A failure never fails the run**: the provider keeps its previous icon (or its monogram, if it never had one), and is retried at the next sync (`icon_source` still differs from `icon_url`).
- **A provider the catalog no longer gives an icon** loses `icon_url`, `icon_asset` and `icon_source` at the merge, and shows its **monogram**: the initials of its name on the color of its first label, in black or white, whichever contrasts more.
- The run report's `icons` section lists what was fetched, how many were already current, and every refusal with its reason.
- Logos remain third-party trademarks: they are displayed to identify a service in a private application and are not redistributed.

### Plan suggestions

- `recurrency` maps to a duration: `monthly` → `{1, month}`, `yearly` → `{1, year}`, `weekly` → `{1, week}`, `daily` → `{1, day}`.
- Catalog prices have no date and no region. They only prefill the "add subscription" form, marked as indicative. A `BillingPhase` is created only from what the user validates.

## Timeline algorithm

Builds coverage periods and charges for one subscription.

1. Collect the subscription's phases and the grants of all purchases that reference it.
2. Walk time forward, keeping `coverage_end` = end of the last coverage period built so far (initially none).
3. **Grants**, processed in order of their purchase date:
   - If `starts_on` is set, the period is `[starts_on, starts_on + duration)`.
   - If `starts_on` is `null`, the period **stacks**: `start = max(purchase.date, coverage_end)`, `end = start + duration`.
4. **Phases**: whenever the subscription is not covered at date `t` (coverage ended, or never started), and a phase is active at `t`, and `auto_renew` is true, and `t < cancelled_at` (if set), and `max_cycles` is not reached:
   - emit a charge on `t` for `price`,
   - emit a `paid` coverage period `[t, t + covers)`.
   If the phase starts after `coverage_end`, the first charge is on `phase.starts_on` (the gap is uncovered time).
5. Grants and phase renewals are interleaved chronologically: a grant purchased while a phase is running stacks after the current renewal, and the phase's next charge moves to the end of the stacked coverage.
6. Stop at the requested horizon (today for history, a future date for forecasting).

Past charges derived from phases are assumed to have happened as described. Future ones are projections.

## Derived computations

- **Is covered at date d**: some coverage period contains `d`.
- **Expiry date**: end of the last coverage period.
- **Next charge**: the next charge a running phase would emit (start of the first uncovered day), if `auto_renew` and not cancelled.
- **Cash cost over [a, b)**: sum of charges whose date falls in `[a, b)`. Bundles count their full price here (it is real money), but can be reported separately.
- **Amortized cost over [a, b)**: for each charge, take its cost basis (`allocated_amount` for bundles, `price` otherwise), divide it by the total number of days of the coverage periods it produced, and add the daily rate for every covered day inside `[a, b)`. Bonus and gift days produced by the same purchase lower the daily rate. A received gift (`price 0`) contributes nothing.
- **Effective monthly cost**: amortized cost over a calendar month.
- **Free days**: days covered by `bonus`, `included`, `trial` or `gift` periods, useful to report the value of promotions.

## How offers map onto the model

| Real-world offer | Model |
|---|---|
| 10 €/month, auto-renewed | 1 `Subscription` + 1 `BillingPhase { price 10, every {1, month} }` |
| Price increase | Close the current phase (`ends_on`), open a new one with the new price. Same subscription. |
| Pay 12 months, get 28 free | `Purchase { kind: prepaid_term, grants: [{12 month, paid}, {28 month, bonus}] }` |
| Buy an extension, 2 months included | `Purchase { kind: bundle, allocated_amount: 0 or fair value, grants: [{2 month, included}] }` |
| Gift card of X months while subscription is running | `Purchase { kind: gift, price 0, payer external, grants: [{X month, gift, starts_on: null}] }`. Stacks after current coverage; the next phase charge moves back by X months. |
| Gift card bought for yourself (e.g. discounted) | Same, with the real `price` and your `payer`. Amortized over the X months. |
| Free trial | `Purchase { kind: prepaid_term, price 0, grants: [{1 month, trial}] }`, or a phase with price 0 and `max_cycles: 1` followed by the paid phase |
| Intro promo (3 months at 0.99, then 9.99) | Phase `{ price 0.99, max_cycles 3 }`, then phase `{ price 9.99 }` starting when the first ends |
| Irregular period to align renewal dates | `Purchase { kind: prepaid_term, grants: [{4 month, paid}] }` |
| Refund or pro-rata credit | `Purchase { kind: refund or adjustment, price negative, grants: [] }` |
| Family plan shared by several users | Core ownership = both adults at 50/50, `service_users` = members, `payer` on each phase/purchase |

### Out of scope: money credit cards

A store credit card worth an amount (e.g. "25 € Apple / Battle.net balance") does **not** grant time. It is a balance that pays for future charges. It should be modeled as a payment method or wallet balance, not as a grant, so charges keep their normal price and the cost is recorded when the credit was bought. Not covered by this model yet.

## Validation rules

- Ids are unique per entity.
- `ends_on > starts_on` for phases; phases of one subscription do not overlap.
- Grant durations have `count > 0`.
- `allocated_amount` is only set on bundles and satisfies `0 <= allocated_amount <= price`.
- Negative prices only on `adjustment` / `refund`.
- Grants with an explicit `starts_on` that overlap existing coverage produce a **warning** (possible duplicate entry), not an error.
- A subscription with no phase, no purchase and no coverage is flagged as incomplete.

## Migration from the previous format

The previous format stored one record per period with `friendly_name`, `custom_price`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `recurrency` and `custom_recurrency`.

| Old | New |
|---|---|
| Several records for the same service | One `Subscription`. The converter proposes a grouping by `provider_key` + `friendly_name` + `owner`; the final grouping is confirmed in a reviewed grouping file (see the issues below). |
| Record with `recurrency` monthly/quarterly/half_yearly/yearly/custom | `BillingPhase` with `every` = `{1, month}`, `{3, month}`, `{6, month}`, `{1, year}`, `{custom_recurrency, month}` |
| Record with `recurrency: one_time` and a start/end range | `Purchase { kind: prepaid_term }` with a `paid` grant matching the range |
| `custom_price` | `price` |
| `end_date` | `ends_on`, converted to half-open (inclusive ends like `2025-03-27` become `2025-03-28`) |
| missing `payer` | Defaults to the owner: a `person` for a personal owner, `household` for a family owner |
| `owner: { type: personal }` | Core ownership: 100 % to the matching person. Visibility `shared` by default; candidates for `private` are reviewed. |
| `owner: { type: family }` | Core ownership: both adults at 50/50, visibility `shared` |
| `service_users` | `person` ids of the Core |
| Family member of type `kid` (no login) | Core `person` of kind `dependent` |
| `provider_key` such as `s_spotify` | Catalog key without the `s_` prefix (`spotify`). A key with no catalog match creates a `custom` provider from `friendly_name` and is listed in the migration report. |

Known data issues to review during migration:

- Duplicate ids across different periods (JetBrains `b426d65e…`, WoW `e90c2132…`).
- Overlapping Spotify records starting on 2023-08-28 (ending 2024-05-28 and 2024-06-28).
- WoW records with inconsistent price/period (a "1 Month" at 35.97 €, a "3 Month" at 12.99 €) and quarterly records spanning 15 months, probably year typos in `end_date`.
- `payer: { type: family }` without an id.
- The grouping key is not enough: records of one subscription do not share a `friendly_name` (World of Warcraft appears under four names), and two products of one provider share `provider_key` and owner (JetBrains Ultimate and JetBrains AI). The migration needs a reviewed grouping file.
- Mixed date conventions: some chains use an inclusive end date (last covered day), others a half-open end. Adjacency alone does not tell them apart (a one-day gap can be a real lapse): classify a chain by its month arithmetic. A yearly record ending one day before its anniversary uses an inclusive end.
- A record with `recurrency: monthly` or `quarterly` that spans several periods stands for **several charges**, one per period, not for one payment. For a subscription paid by hand each time, expand it into one purchase per period.
- The NordVPN term starting in June 2025 ends 15 days later than 24 + 6 months from its start. Check whether extra bonus days were granted.
- Payments in USD (2018 and 2020) predate the exchange-rate data (see `core-data-model.md` §13).
- `recurrency: one_time` covers prepaid terms and a bundle. The bundle cannot be detected automatically.
- Datetimes are stored as `…T00:00:00Z`: convert to calendar dates.
- `recurrency: custom` with `custom_recurrency: 4` is a 4-month irregular period: a `prepaid_term` purchase.

The converted data goes into the standard import file described in `import-format.md`.
